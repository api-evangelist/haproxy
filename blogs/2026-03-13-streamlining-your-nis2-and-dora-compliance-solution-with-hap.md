                <h2 id="the-eus-cybersecurity-mandate-is-in-effect-heres-how-haproxy-supports-your-regulatory-requirements">The EU&#039;s cybersecurity mandate is in effect. Here’s how HAProxy supports your regulatory requirements.</h2><p>With NIS2 and DORA now in effect, EU organizations face a fundamental shift in how they approach security. Compliance is a standard built into every layer of your environment, from your hardware and OS to your software configuration.</p><p>HAProxy alone doesn&#039;t make an organization compliant, yet it serves as a critical technical component of a strong security strategy. By providing multi-layered security at the application layer, HAProxy Enterprise helps teams meet the technical expectations of these mandates as part of their broader security infrastructure.</p>
                
            
                
            
                <h2 id="nis2-vs-dora-what-do-they-require">NIS2 vs DORA: what do they require?</h2><p>The European Union introduced <strong>NIS2 and DORA</strong> with a clear mission: to protect the essential services that EU citizens rely on every day. From power grids and telecommunications to local governments and banking systems, these critical sectors are increasingly targeted by ransomware, <a href="http://production.int.haproxy.com/glossary/what-is-a-distributed-denial-of-service-ddos-attack">DDoS attacks</a>, and other sophisticated threats.</p><p>Both frameworks mandate that organizations raise their cybersecurity and operational resilience standards to ensure they remain secure, reliable, and operationally stable.</p><h3 id="nis2-strengthening-cybersecurity-across-essential-service">NIS2: strengthening cybersecurity across essential service<strong>s</strong></h3><p>NIS2 (Network and Information Security Directive 2) builds upon the original NIS Directive, introducing stricter requirements and encompassing a broader range of sectors. </p><p>It mandates that organizations implement <strong>appropriate and proportionate technical, operational, and organizational measures</strong> to manage cybersecurity risks.</p><p>In practice, this means:</p><ul><li><p>Conducting regular <strong>risk assessments</strong> to find and fix vulnerabilities.</p></li><li><p>Establishing transparent processes for <strong>incident detection and reporting</strong> within strict deadlines.</p></li><li><p>Ensuring <strong>business continuity</strong> and recovery capabilities.</p></li><li><p>Managing <strong>supply chain security</strong> and implementing safeguards such as encryption and access control.</p></li><li><p>Making <strong>executive management accountable</strong> for security decisions.</p></li></ul><p>NIS2 applies to critical entities in industries such as energy, transport, healthcare, digital infrastructure, <a href="http://production.int.haproxy.com/solutions/government-public-sector">public administration</a>, food production, and manufacturing.</p><h3 id="dora-what-financial-institutions-must-prove">DORA: what financial institutions must prove</h3><p>While NIS2 casts a wide net, the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) focuses specifically on financial institutions and their technology providers.</p><p>DORA requires a comprehensive framework for operational resilience, ensuring financial entities can withstand, respond to, and recover from all information and communication technology (ICT) related incidents. To comply, organizations must:</p><ul><li><p>Establish a formal <strong>framework for managing information and communication technology risks</strong>.</p></li><li><p>Implement <strong>continuous monitoring</strong> and threat intelligence.</p></li><li><p>Conduct advanced <strong>digital operational resilience testing</strong>.</p></li><li><p>Maintain robust <strong>incident classification and reporting</strong>.</p></li><li><p>Oversee and audit <strong>critical third-party providers</strong>.</p></li></ul><p>This regulation affects banks, insurers, investment firms, payment processors, crypto-asset providers, and the cloud or information and communication technology vendors that serve them.</p><h3 id="what-are-the-penalties-under-nis2-and-dora">What are the penalties under NIS2 and DORA?</h3><p>Non-compliance penalties under NIS2 and DORA are intentionally severe to ensure high-level accountability. </p><p>The EU has established one of the most stringent accountability frameworks with the NIS2 and DORA regulations. Non-compliance with NIS2 can result in fines reaching up to <strong>€10 million or 2% of global turnover</strong>. Similarly, DORA imposes fines of up to <strong>2%, with the potential for daily penalties for continuous non-compliance</strong>.</p><h2 id="summary-of-nis2-and-dora-requirements">Summary of NIS2 and DORA requirements</h2><table><tbody><tr><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p></p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p><strong>NIS2</strong></p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p><strong>DORA</strong></p></td></tr><tr><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p><strong>Who it applies to</strong></p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>Essential and important entities in sectors such as energy, healthcare, transport, public administration, digital infrastructure, and manufacturing.</p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>Financial institutions and ICT third-party providers, including banks, insurers, investment firms, and crypto-asset service providers.</p></td></tr><tr><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p><strong>Management liability and sanctions</strong></p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>• Executives (CEOs, board members) can be <strong>personally liable</strong> for gross negligence.
